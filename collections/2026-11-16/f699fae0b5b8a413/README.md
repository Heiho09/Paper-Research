### [Discovery and Development of Asymmetric Autocatalysis](https://doi.org/10.1246/bcsj.77.1063)

Kenso Soai, Takanori Shibata, Itaru Sato · 2004 · Bulletin of the Chemical Society of Japan

Abstract Asymmetric autocatalysis occurs when a chiral product operates as an asymmetric catalyst for its own production. We have discovered an asymmetric autocatalytic reaction in the enantioselective addition of dialkylzinc to pyridine-3-carbaldehyde, where the product, 3-pyridyl alkanol, acts as an asymmetric autocatalyst. A diol, ferrocenyl alkanol, 5-pyrimidyl alkanol, and 3-quinolyl alkanol were also found to be asymmetric autocatalysts for the enantioselective isopropylation of the corresponding aldehydes. Of these compounds, 5-(2-alkynyl)pyrimidyl alkanol was the best asymmetric autocatalyst, and the asymmetric autocatalytic isopropylation of the corresponding pyrimidine-5-carbaldehyde proceeded quantitatively ( &amp;gt;99%), affording itself as a near enantiomerically pure ( &amp;gt;99.5% ee) chiral product. When an asymmetric autocatalyst with an extremely low ee was used, the asymmetric autocatalytic reaction proceeded along with amplification of the ee, and a highly enantiomerically enriched product was obtained. When the enantioselective isopropylation of pyrimidine-5-carbaldehydes was examined in the presence of chiral organic compounds with a very low ee acting as chiral initiators, then highly enantiomerically enriched 5-pyrimidyl alkanols were obtained. Their absolute configurations were dependent upon the slight excess of enantiomer in the chiral initiators. Chiral inorganic crystals, such as quartz and sodium chlorate, also act as chiral initiators. In conjunction with asymmetric autocatalysis, the chirality of organic compounds with a high enantiomeric excess has been correlated for the first time with the circularly polarized light and chiral inorganic crystals. Moreover, when an enantioselective isopropylation was examined in the absence of a chiral initiator, asymmetric induction above the detection level was observed in the product, with the ratio of the resulting predominant S- and R-isomers being almost equal. This result fulfills one of the conditions necessary for spontaneous absolute asymmetric synthesis.

### [Role of Asymmetric Autocatalysis in the Elucidation of Origins of Homochirality of Organic Compounds](https://doi.org/10.3390/sym11050694)

Kenso Soai, Tsuneomi Kawasaki, Arimasa Matsumoto · 2019 · Symmetry

Pyrimidyl alkanol and related compounds were found to be asymmetric autocatalysts in the enantioselective addition of diisopropylzinc to pyrimidine-5-carbaldehyde and related aldehydes. In the asymmetric autocatalysis with amplification of enantiomeric excess (ee), the very low ee (ca. 0.00005%) of 2-alkynyl-5-pyrimidyl alkanol was significantly amplified to &gt;99.5% ee with an increase in the amount. By using asymmetric autocatalysis with amplification of ee, several origins of homochirality have been examined. Circularly polarized light, chiral quartz, and chiral crystals formed from achiral organic compounds such as glycine and carbon (13C/12C), nitrogen (15N/14N), oxygen (18O/16O), and hydrogen (D/H) chiral isotopomers were found to act as the origin of chirality in asymmetric autocatalysis. And the spontaneous absolute asymmetric synthesis was also realized without the intervention of any chiral factor.

### [Training Reasoning Models in Simulation to Natively Use Real Robots](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18562)

Jiaming Tang, Zhuoyang Zhang, Junxian Guo · 2026-11-13 · arXiv

Robot-use agents (RUAs) have emerged as a promising approach to general-purpose robot control. However, strong performance has so far been achieved only by frontier proprietary models such as GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.2, whereas open-weight models remain substantially less capable. We show that an open-weight 27B reasoning model can be trained to use real robots natively through reinforcement learning, entirely in simulation and without demonstrations. Like a computer-use agent (CUA), the model observes the environment, reasons in a chain of thought, and generates the next end-effector pose, without any harness. We build a Post-training Engine for Agentic Robotics (PEAR) and use it to train the model on simulated robot tasks at scale with robot-use reinforcement learning (RURL), mirroring how frontier models acquire computer use. We find that the skills acquired in simulation generalize well beyond the training tasks: they transfer to an unseen robot, to the real world, and to novel instructions. After training on LIBERO and RoboDojo in simulation, the model succeeds in 60% of our trials on a real dual-arm robot, whereas the base model succeeds in none. On LIBERO, training raises the success rate from 1.6% to 74.4%, approaching GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol at maximum reasoning effort. Compared with $π\_{0.5}$, a vision-language-action model fine-tuned on demonstrations, our model is substantially more robust to changes in the task itself. On LIBERO-Pro, $π\_{0.5}$ succeeds in only 0.75% of episodes when assigned a new task in a familiar scene whereas our model succeeds in 57.4%. Finally, the model retains its general capabilities, with no measurable degradation on nine reasoning, coding and vision benchmarks. These results highlight robot-use reinforcement learning as a promising component of foundation-model post-training.

### [Modeling the Frictional Contact of Continuum Robots with Environments through Linear Complementarity Problem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18546)

Jia Shen, Junhyoung Ha, Yue Chen · 2026-11-13 · arXiv

Accurate friction modeling in continuum robots is essential for precise motion control and path planning. However, current approaches make predictions only for the mechanism&#x27;s internal friction that is limited by the boundary forces of the Coulomb friction cone, which fail to characterize the static friction and motion hysteresis. In this paper, we propose a novel friction model based on complementarity constraints under the Coulomb friction assumption. This model predicts various friction effects caused by environmental interactions, including both static and sliding frictions and hysteresis behavior, as well as the corresponding robot deformation induced by these interactions, which are crucial for the practical motion planning and control of continuum robots. Additionally, real-time implementation of this model can be achieved, meeting the demand for computational efficiency in robotic applications. Experimental validations demonstrated that this model is able to reduce the maximum tip position error from 45.4 mm in the frictionless model to 5.85 mm in our friction-included model with a 287.9 mm-long tendon-driven continuum robot.

### [Disturbance-Aware Informative Planning in Spatiotemporal Environments](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18505)

Kaleb Ben Naveed, Haejoon Lee, Anouck Girard · 2026-11-13 · arXiv

Autonomous robots are widely used for persistent monitoring of spatiotemporal environments, where quantities of interest (QoI) change across space and time and require repeated measurements. In practice, robot motion can be affected by external disturbances that vary across the environment. When such external disturbances are not considered in trajectory optimization, it can lead to inaccurate predictions of robot motion and, consequently, the information acquired about the QoI during execution. However, estimating disturbances throughout the environment can be inefficient, as disturbances in regions with little overlap with monitoring trajectories have limited impact on monitoring performance. We therefore propose a two-stage informative planning framework for unknown, spatially varying but time-invariant disturbances, combining informed disturbance sampling with disturbance-aware trajectory optimization. First, we identify where QoI measurements are needed and generate alternative paths to these locations using the environment geometry. We then prioritize disturbance measurements based on their expected reduction of disturbance uncertainty along these paths. Second, rather than optimizing information acquisition based on nominal robot motion, we incorporate the learned disturbance belief into trajectory optimization to account for its effects on robot motion and information acquisition. Across four simulation scenarios, the proposed method reduces disturbance-learning time by 25.0--52.3 percent compared with environment-wide estimation and identifies paths that appear costly under nominal dynamics but become preferable when disturbances are considered.

### [In Situ Monitoring and Predictive Modeling of Harmful Algal Blooms with Autonomous Surface Vehicles](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18501)

Nenyi Kweku Nkensen Dadson, Jean Paul Gonzalez, Daniel P. Arabie · 2026-11-13 · arXiv

Harmful Algal Blooms (HABs) pose significant environmental, economic, and public-health challenges in coastal and inland water bodies, creating a need for scalable methods to collect spatially distributed water-quality data. The results demonstrate the feasibility of using a modular ASV platform for repeatable, multi-modal water-quality data collection and support future development of autonomous monitoring systems.

### [Toward Resilient Self-Driving Laboratories through Telerobotic Interventions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18496)

Ziheng Wu, Adrian Vecina Tercero, Andre A. Fatehi · 2026-11-13 · arXiv

Self-driving laboratories (SDLs) accelerate scientific research by integrating robotics, automated experimentation, and machine learning to execute experiments autonomously. Biology and chemistry have adopted these systems. However, increasing SDL complexity heightens susceptibility to unexpected faults in automated workflows, highlighting the need for efficient recovery mechanisms. In this study, we investigate telerobotic interventions to enhance SDL resilience against unexpected failures. We developed a teleoperation interface enabling human operators to resolve disruptions during automated tasks. The interface enables precise control of a six-degree-of-freedom (DOF) robotic arm (UR3e) using a 3D mouse (3Dconnexion SpaceMouse Pro). Two velocity-based controller strategies were evaluated: (i) a coupled mode mapping all six DOFs to the 3D mouse&#x27;s central knob and (ii) a decoupled mode separating translational and rotational motions. To evaluate the framework, teleoperators intervened across three SDL fault scenarios. In the first study, comparative trials showed that the decoupled controller was associated with shorter completion times and reduced rotational motion. In a second study, teleoperators performed an aiming task with and without reference-path guidance. Users were more target-driven and risk-tolerant without guidance but showed behavioral differences under different guidance strategies. These findings suggest that controller configuration and reference-path design influence operator performance during SDL fault recovery, informing teleoperation interface optimization. This work demonstrates the potential feasibility of teleoperation as a human-in-the-loop recovery mechanism for SDL workflows, combining robotic precision with human adaptability and problem-solving. These findings also provide a foundation for future studies in shared autonomy and semi-autonomous intervention.

### [When Will You Hand It Over? A Hidden Start Delay Makes Self-Play Handover Robust to Unfamiliar Partners](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18491)

Mohammad Khoshnazar, Mike Degany, Deyuan Qu · 2026-11-13 · arXiv

Cooperative self-play can teach a humanoid to receive an object without any human data, but the partner it meets after training is never the one it trained with; we show that a fluent self-play handover policy breaks with unfamiliar partners, identify the main cause, and repair it. In a physics-based parcel handover between a Unitree G1 and a learned giver, both trained by alternating PPO self-play, the receiver completes 95% and 90% of handovers with its own partner (two training seeds) but only 31% with scripted partners it has never met. Paired interventions isolate the cause: under a shared reward the learned giver always starts its offer at once, so the receiver learns a single rendezvous, and delaying its own partner by one second cuts success from 95% to 6% (87% to 34% in the second seed). We introduce hidden start-delay randomisation (HSD), a one-line rule on the learned partner that the receiver never observes. After 300 further updates, success with unseen scripted partners rises from 31% to 80% in both seeds, against 35--41% for a budget-matched control. The gain holds on a fresh scenario seed, on eleven held-out partner conditions and on partners that start later than any training delay; it cuts high-force contact three- to five-fold and needs no retraining of the partner.

### [The State Is Not the Bottleneck: A Paged-State Gated-DeltaNet Tile on a Tri-Mode Multicast Mesh, Bit-Exact from RTL to Silicon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18422)

Cong Xu, Ravi Sankar · 2026-11-13 · arXiv

Linear-attention language models replace a growing key-value cache with a recurrent state of fixed size. On an edge FPGA, this state can exceed the on-chip memory and must be paged to external memory. This paper asks whether such paging dominates the cost of decoding (generating tokens). We built a hardware tile that computes one complete layer of a custom 369-million-parameter hybrid Gated DeltaNet model on an Agilex 5 FPGA and pages its recurrent state. The model state needs 6.2 MiB, whereas the device has 0.87 MiB of block memory. Paging adds 26.0 MB of memory traffic per decoded token, at most 11.2 percent of the calculated total. On the board at 75 MHz, with synthetic weights, one token takes 11.15 ms in this layer. The matrix-vector products with the weights take about 62 percent of this time and the state phase, including the paging, 13 percent. The tile is one node of a multicast mesh of tri-mode processing elements. We measure the error of the fixed-point arithmetic against floating point and check that the hardware agrees bit for bit with a fixed-point reference. In simulation, the state logic agrees with the reference on all 18 recurrent layers. On the board, six builds pass all 30 runs of 64 tokens and two runs of 1,024 tokens. For this model and memory interface, the delivery of the weights limits the decoding performance, and the recurrent state does not. State paging is therefore practical for such a model on an edge FPGA.

### [CS-MPC: Contraction-Certified Sampling-Based MPC for Nonlinear Systems Under Uncertainty](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18390)

Yashwanth Kumar Nakka · 2026-11-13 · arXiv

We present contraction-certified sampling-based model predictive control (CS-MPC) for tracking and safety of nonlinear robotic systems under parametric uncertainty, disturbances, and safety constraints that are revealed online. CS-MPC uses a contraction-certified tracking feedback inside every sampled rollout, which concentrates the rollouts around the reference, and an execution layer that verifies the applied input using a quantile-tightened discrete-time control barrier function condition. We derive a verification procedure for nonlinear contraction of the feedback-closed dynamics, and we prove a finite-sample stochastic stability bound and a mission-level probabilistic safety guarantee. We validate CS-MPC in simulation on a rover, a quadrotor, and an asteroid landing. When compared with standard model predictive path integral (MPPI) control, CS-MPC attains the same tracking error with 7 to 20 times fewer rollouts for the rover, and a 2 to 4 times lower tracking error for the quadrotor, with no safety violations in 200 Monte Carlo missions.

### [Vision-Language-Action Models: A Life-Cycle Survey of Design, Training and Deployment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18372)

Suvarna Kadam, Soran Parsa · 2026-11-13 · arXiv

Vision-language-action (VLA) models adapt web-pretrained vision-language models to output robot actions, so that web knowledge carries over to physical control. In under 4 years the field has moved from RT-2, the first VLA, to open models that fine-tune on a single consumer GPU and to proprietary generalists that clean unseen homes. This survey reviews 88 works from December 2022 to October 2026, mainly on manipulation and navigation, plus humanoid control, world-action models and evaluation. We follow a VLA through its life cycle, from model design to training and deployment, along seven axes. Model design covers (a) inputs and memory, what the model observes; (b) architecture, how its pipeline is built; (c) action output, how it produces motor commands; and (d) intermediate predictions, what else it predicts, from reasoning steps to future observations. Training covers (e) pretraining data, the mix of robot, web, human and simulated data, and (f) post-training, adaptation to the target robot. Deployment covers (g) real-time execution, keeping pace with the robot. We analyse 22 models on these axes and report their training data, model size, inference speed and hardware. Three trends emerge: (i) training data has broadened, from about 10,000 hours of teleoperated robot data ($π\_0$) to 23,000 community-collected episodes (SmolVLA) and 20,000 hours of egocentric human video (GR00T N1.7); (ii) model sizes have split: most recent models have 3-7 billion parameters, while a new group under one billion runs on consumer GPUs; and (iii) generalist VLAs are emerging that handle both manipulation and navigation, such as UniVLA and Qwen-VLA. We also compare VLAs with world-action models, which predict future observations as well as actions. We review evidence that some VLAs with near-perfect benchmark scores fail when objects or the camera move slightly, and open up further research questions.

### [Envisioning Agentic Robotics: A New Era of Robotic Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18359)

Grid Robotics,  :, Qingfeng Lan · 2026-11-13 · arXiv

The release of GPT-6 Astra marks the emergence of a new paradigm in robotics: agentic robotics. Within weeks, frontier models with agentic harnesses matched or exceeded task-specific policies on many embodied benchmarks. Instead of training a dedicated policy for each family of tasks, an agentic robotic system applies a foundation model to open-world embodied tasks. It operates in a closed loop: observing the environment, interpreting user intent, reasoning about the task, invoking skills and tools, verifying outcomes, and adapting its strategy. Despite these advances, the community has yet to converge on a shared definition, a reliable evaluation framework, or a research roadmap. We argue that Embodied System 2, realized by a foundation model together with an embodied harness, forms the decision-making core of agentic robotics. Its central challenge is to translate general-purpose reasoning into effective and verifiable actions under physical constraints such as limited reaction time, mechanical limits, unpredictable environment changes, and irreversible consequences. Motivated by this perspective, we propose an evaluation framework that measures both model capabilities and closed-loop system performance. Furthermore, we identify the principles that carry over from digital to physical agents and the challenges that must be addressed to achieve reliable physical autonomy. On top of the Embodied System 2 and the evaluation framework, we envision a research roadmap toward sustained autonomy: the ability to perform work safely and reliably with progressively broader responsibility, from executing individual primitives to fulfilling standing roles.

### [A structured framework for assessment of rehabilitation and sick-listing (STARS) - experiences among healthcare professionals.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42900467/)

Eva-Marie Sundkvist, Inger Jansson, Annette Sverker · 2026-10-20 · Scandinavian journal of primary health care

BACKGROUND/PURPOSE: The sick-listing process is often perceived uncertain and unclear by both patients and staff. To improve the process, a framework for structured assessment of rehabilitation and sick-listing (STARS) has been developed in the South-Eastern Healthcare Region in Sweden. STARS consists of seven methodological tools supporting healthcare professionals in sick-listing and rehabilitation. The purpose of this study was to investigate how primary healthcare staff clinically involved in the sick leave process experience working according to STARS.
METHOD: We used a qualitative design conducting focus group discussions (n = 7) with healthcare staff (n = 37). Eligible participants were physicians, rehabilitation coordinators and other healthcare professionals working clinically with patients. The focus group conversations were analyzed with thematic content analysis according to Braun and Clarke. The study was registered in clinicaltrials.gov (number NCT05655962).
RESULT: The participants&#x27; experience of working according to STARS were described by the overarching theme &#x27;STARS as an enabling structure&#x27; formed by three themes and eight subthemes. The themes were &#x27;Clearer roles and responsibilities through the RECO function&#x27;, &#x27;Moving from individual work to teamwork&#x27; and &#x27;A more active and personalized rehabilitation and sick-listing process&#x27;.

We define the scope through evaluation-directed checks and the use of their results, covering both structured workflows and observation-driven control. We organize the design space by multi-agent collaboration, tool integration, memory, and planning, and show how these mechanisms supply the checks and evidence on which a verdict rests. We consolidate published scores and reproduce missing entries for key agentic judges; the comparison reveals uneven agentic gains and motivates dedicated agentic evaluation. We conclude by characterizing application demands and open challenges in cost, reliability, safety, and governance that condition trusted deployment of agentic judges at scale.

### [From Images to Grounded Edits: A Real-World Benchmark for Chinese Grammatical Error Correction in Handwritten Essays](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17966)

Weiru Zhang, Yuzhong Hong, Junwei Bao · 2026-11-13 · arXiv

Chinese Grammatical Error Correction (CGEC) is typically formulated as a text-to-text problem. While suitable for digital texts, this formulation falls short in real-world K-12 education, where AI-assisted corrections are expected directly on essay images, which are primarily handwritten and exhibit many irregularities. To bridge this gap, we introduce Grounded Chinese Grammatical Error Correction (G-CGEC), an image-to-grounded-edit task requiring systems to predict both corrections and their positions on essay images. We propose G-CGEC-Eval, a benchmark comprising real-world images of K-12 Chinese essays with fine-grained annotations. For evaluation, we adapt IoU-based metrics from object detection and propose a more tolerant alternative based on center-distance matching. Using this benchmark, we evaluate open-source and proprietary vision-language models under a direct single-pass setting, as well as settings with auxiliary components (OCR and character grounding) coordinated via pipelining or agentic harnessing. Results show that even top-tier proprietary models perform surprisingly poorly in the single-pass setting, while pipelining and agentic harnessing yield meaningful improvements. Notably, an error-type-based multi-agent workflow achieves the best performance, establishing it as the strongest baseline for future G-CGEC research. Through ablations and fine-grained analysis, we find the task remains far from solved, with linguistic error discovery and character-level grounding as the primary bottlenecks. This underscores the need for models that better integrate visual perception with linguistic reasoning and visual grounding.

### [MineralExp: Benchmarking LLM Agents for Experimental Design in Real-World Mineral Exploration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17931)

Bing Wang, Mingyang Wang, Yuanhang Mi · 2026-11-13 · arXiv

In mineral exploration, verifying geological hypotheses relies on designing a variety of geochemical experiments. While large language model (LLM) agents increasingly automate downstream geoscientific data analysis, a crucial upstream capability remains largely unexplored: automated geochemical experimental design, i.e., deciding which experiments to conduct before committing to costly laboratory work. Evaluating this capability of LLM agents is difficult for two reasons. First, geochemical experiments exhibit high methodological diversity and subjectivity: to test the same hypothesis, different geologists may choose different minerals and instruments, so no single protocol can serve as the reference answer. Second, open-ended experimental plans resist representation as structured labels, which makes objective scoring difficult. To address these challenges, we introduce MineralExp, a benchmark that reformulates geochemical experimental design as a hypothesis-based experiment selection problem, i.e., a multi-label classification problem. We abstract concrete instrument names and procedural details into a shared taxonomy of 19 experimental types across five mineral-system processes: Source, Transport, Deposition, Modification, and Preservation. This taxonomy yields deterministic multi-label targets while accommodating diverse experimental protocols. Constructed from published ore-deposit literature and verified by domain experts, MineralExp comprises 277 cases and 1,572 reference experimental plans. Given a geological hypothesis and initial context, an LLM agent selects the relevant tasks and generates concrete experimental plans, which are scored by multi-label classification metrics and an LLM-as-a-Judge metric. The experimental results show that current agents generally achieve high recall but low precision, tending to propose more experiments than are necessary to test the hypothesis.
