### [Role of Asymmetric Autocatalysis in the Elucidation of Origins of Homochirality of Organic Compounds](https://doi.org/10.3390/sym11050694)

Kenso Soai, Tsuneomi Kawasaki, Arimasa Matsumoto · 2019 · Symmetry

Pyrimidyl alkanol and related compounds were found to be asymmetric autocatalysts in the enantioselective addition of diisopropylzinc to pyrimidine-5-carbaldehyde and related aldehydes. In the asymmetric autocatalysis with amplification of enantiomeric excess (ee), the very low ee (ca. 0.00005%) of 2-alkynyl-5-pyrimidyl alkanol was significantly amplified to &gt;99.5% ee with an increase in the amount. By using asymmetric autocatalysis with amplification of ee, several origins of homochirality have been examined. Circularly polarized light, chiral quartz, and chiral crystals formed from achiral organic compounds such as glycine and carbon (13C/12C), nitrogen (15N/14N), oxygen (18O/16O), and hydrogen (D/H) chiral isotopomers were found to act as the origin of chirality in asymmetric autocatalysis. And the spontaneous absolute asymmetric synthesis was also realized without the intervention of any chiral factor.

### [Discovery and Development of Asymmetric Autocatalysis](https://doi.org/10.1246/bcsj.77.1063)

Kenso Soai, Takanori Shibata, Itaru Sato · 2004 · Bulletin of the Chemical Society of Japan

Abstract Asymmetric autocatalysis occurs when a chiral product operates as an asymmetric catalyst for its own production. We have discovered an asymmetric autocatalytic reaction in the enantioselective addition of dialkylzinc to pyridine-3-carbaldehyde, where the product, 3-pyridyl alkanol, acts as an asymmetric autocatalyst. A diol, ferrocenyl alkanol, 5-pyrimidyl alkanol, and 3-quinolyl alkanol were also found to be asymmetric autocatalysts for the enantioselective isopropylation of the corresponding aldehydes. Of these compounds, 5-(2-alkynyl)pyrimidyl alkanol was the best asymmetric autocatalyst, and the asymmetric autocatalytic isopropylation of the corresponding pyrimidine-5-carbaldehyde proceeded quantitatively ( &amp;gt;99%), affording itself as a near enantiomerically pure ( &amp;gt;99.5% ee) chiral product. When an asymmetric autocatalyst with an extremely low ee was used, the asymmetric autocatalytic reaction proceeded along with amplification of the ee, and a highly enantiomerically enriched product was obtained. When the enantioselective isopropylation of pyrimidine-5-carbaldehydes was examined in the presence of chiral organic compounds with a very low ee acting as chiral initiators, then highly enantiomerically enriched 5-pyrimidyl alkanols were obtained. Their absolute configurations were dependent upon the slight excess of enantiomer in the chiral initiators. Chiral inorganic crystals, such as quartz and sodium chlorate, also act as chiral initiators. In conjunction with asymmetric autocatalysis, the chirality of organic compounds with a high enantiomeric excess has been correlated for the first time with the circularly polarized light and chiral inorganic crystals. Moreover, when an enantioselective isopropylation was examined in the absence of a chiral initiator, asymmetric induction above the detection level was observed in the product, with the ratio of the resulting predominant S- and R-isomers being almost equal. This result fulfills one of the conditions necessary for spontaneous absolute asymmetric synthesis.

### [$Z$-sight: Unifying Vision-Language-Action and Latent World Modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24940)

Junjie Ye, Yiqi Zhao, Muchen Xu · 2026-11-18 · arXiv

We introduce $Z$-sight, a unified robot foundation model that couples vision-language-action (VLA) and generative latent world modeling. Video-based world action models (WAMs) show the value of joint future-and-action generation for control, yet inherit the heavy cost of video generators. Recent latent-space policies avoid pixel generation, yet typically align intermediate features or rely on frozen target encoders, decoupling future prediction from the VLM&#x27;s native feature space. $Z$-sight instead predicts the future directly in the visual token space in which its VLM perceives the present. Coupling a foresight expert and a continuous action expert with the VLM in a mixture-of-transformers, $Z$-sight jointly denoises future spatial tokens and actions at inference without pixel reconstruction or external encoders. Trained on human video and robot trajectories, $Z$-sight achieves 81.9% success on SIMPLE (+9.6 points over the strongest baseline) and 73.3% across six Unitree G1 tasks (+20.0 points) at 110 ms per chunk, while remaining competitive on tabletop benchmarks.

### [Phoenix: Learning Scene Structure Representations for Generalizable World-Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24938)

Yupeng Zheng, Chaoyue Li, Linbo Wang · 2026-11-18 · arXiv

World-action models jointly predict future observations and actions, yet reconstruction-oriented visual representations do not explicitly emphasize the spatial relationships and object-state changes needed for manipulation. We introduce Phoenix, which learns representations of scene structure for world-action modeling. Specifically, Phoenix Encoder enriches DINOv3&#x27;s general visual semantics through dual-teacher distillation of geometric and spatiotemporal semantic knowledge. Next, CS-VAE causally aggregates and compresses Phoenix Encoder features into generative latents while preserving scene information and spatial layout. Finally, Phoenix jointly predicts these latents, complementary appearance latents, and robot actions, linking scene evolution with action generation. To study pretraining data efficiency, we curate approximately 39,000 hours of egocentric and robot manipulation data for representation learning and use the robot demonstration subset for WAM pretraining. Pretraining-scale analysis shows larger performance gains from data scaling than a video-VAE-only baseline, indicating stronger scaling benefits and more effective data utilization. Moreover, Phoenix maintains high success rates when adapted with limited downstream demonstrations, demonstrating strong few-shot adaptation. Our code, pretraining data, and models are available on the project page to help the community reproduce our results.

### [Grana: Compositional Reward Modeling for Long-Horizon Robot Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24926)

Zi-ang Cao, Kaiyuan Zheng, Yinzhen Xu · 2026-11-18 · arXiv

Robot reward modeling faces two challenges: generalizing across tasks and accurately tracking progress in long-horizon manipulation. General-purpose reward models demonstrate broad task transfer, but transfer from short-task pretraining to long-horizon tasks remains underexplored. Long-horizon progress models often rely on task-specific subtask classifiers or fixed action vocabularies, limiting the reuse of learned progress knowledge when adapting to new tasks. To address these challenges, we introduce Grana, a vision-language robot reward model that learns to identify unfinished subtasks and estimate their continuous progress using open-vocabulary subtask descriptions. We train a language-conditioned model to estimate progress in short-horizon tasks and individual subtasks of long-horizon tasks. For long-horizon tasks, we combine the selected subtask&#x27;s predicted progress with credit for preceding completed subtasks to estimate whole-task progress. General progress prediction requires training the model across diverse tasks with progress supervision that accounts for imperfect execution. Elapsed-time proxies can misrepresent progress when relative subtask durations vary, or failures reverse local progress. We therefore introduce an annotation protocol that converts sparse subtask boundaries and action-quality annotations into dense progress targets, without requiring annotators to assign numerical progress scores. Pretraining with this supervision on 200 diverse, mostly short-horizon manipulation tasks enables zero-shot reward prediction on unseen tasks and improves progress prediction after adaptation to multi-minute, long-horizon tasks. Experiments demonstrate (i) generalizable reward prediction, including adaptation to five long-horizon tasks, (ii) benefits from pretraining and composing subtask progress estimates, and (iii) improved downstream policy performance.

### [Communications-free Collective Intelligence Via Allocentric Belief](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24858)

Cameron Mehlman, Arjun Mulchandani, Hovik Grigoryan · 2026-11-18 · arXiv

A team of skilled card players share no information amongst each other, and yet still are able to coordinate their play by watching the same players and reading the same tells. Autonomous systems have failed to exploit this logic, relying instead on explicit communication or complex state estimation schemes that can fail when it matters most. Without it, decentralized agents diverge in their world beliefs, and coordination collapses. Here we show that anchoring all agents to a single shared external actor, a common witness whose reactions encode team state, produces spontaneous, communication-free coordination. An allocentric belief network trained on witness behavior allows each agent to independently reconstruct full team state, keeping group beliefs consistent with no inter-agent exchange. Across five competitive environments allocentric belief alignment outperforms egocentric approaches in collective performance, and even scales to sixty-agent teams where standard methods fail. These results establish a new principle for collective intelligence: in the absence of communication, a shared gaze replaces a shared channel.

### [Does the Action Matter? Probing Action-Conditioned Semantic Forecasting as a World Model for Autonomous Driving](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24840)

Binay Kumar Singh, Niels Da Vitoria Lobo · 2026-11-18 · arXiv

World models are increasingly used in autonomous driving to predict how a scene evolves under the ego vehicle&#x27;s actions, supporting counterfactual reasoning and model-based planning. However, existing evaluations focus on prediction fidelity and planning performance, and rarely verify that the predictions depend on the conditioning action. In this paper, we study action dependence in action-conditioned semantic forecasting, which predicts future semantic segmentation from past segmentations and the ego action. We compare a forecaster with feature-wise and input-level action conditioning against an identical model without action input, and measure action dependence with test-time action interventions scored by standard segmentation metrics. The evaluation is validated in a synthetic environment with known action-dependent dynamics and applied to Cityscapes and BDD100K, both within each dataset and across the two vehicle platforms. These results show that an accuracy gain indicates that a driving world model uses its action, but not how strongly its forecasts depend on it. ### [DiWALI: Diffusion Policy Learning in World Models via Latent Action Inference from Human and Robot Play](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24836)

Akshay L Chandra, Iman Nematollahi, Lingyue Zhao · 2026-11-18 · arXiv

Learning robot manipulation is constrained by the availability of action-labeled robot data, for both imitation and world models. Humans can demonstrate skills in seconds, but their demonstrations lack the required robot actions. We introduce DiWALI, a framework that learns and improves diffusion policies within a world model trained on human and robot play. DiWALI infers latent actions from consecutive frames using an inverse dynamics model, enabling world model training without action labels, and grounds them in robot commands through a decoder trained separately on robot play. Together, these results demonstrate that human experience can support both manipulation skill acquisition and subsequent policy improvement, reducing reliance on action-labeled robot data. ### [Planner-Controller Interaction for Ackermann Navigation: A Matched Simulation and Hardware Benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24800)

Global planners and path-tracking controllers are often evaluated independently, although their interaction determines how a vehicle follows a planned path. Whether simulation accurately predicts the performance of specific planner-controller combinations on Ackermann-steered hardware remains uncertain. This study evaluated three geometric controllers (Regulated Pure Pursuit, Stanley, and Vector Pursuit) paired with two global planners (Hybrid A\* and NavFn) on a 1/10-scale Quanser QCar and in a matched Gazebo simulation. Both environments used shared custom planning and control algorithms, matched route geometry and goals, ground-truth pose references, and a common evaluation pipeline. The findings demonstrate that agreement in controller rankings does not guarantee accurate prediction of planner-dependent performance for specific controller pairings on hardware, highlighting the importance of physical validation.
